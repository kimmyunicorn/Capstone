Harbor & Pool Testing

The SeaSTAR ROV underwent multiple stages of validation before the final demonstration. Initial testing was performed in a controlled pool environment to verify propulsion, controller communication, telemetry transmission, and subsystem integration. Final validation was then conducted in a real harbor environment, where the ROV operated under realistic underwater conditions.

Lessons Learned

Developing SeaSTAR required close collaboration between the mechanical, electrical, embedded, and software teams. Throughout the project, numerous engineering challenges were encountered, including waterproofing electronics, integrating multiple ROS2 nodes, improving communication reliability, and designing an intuitive operator interface.

Iterative testing proved essential throughout development. Each pool test identified new improvements that ultimately increased the reliability and usability of the final system. These iterations strengthened subsystem integration while reinforcing the importance of modular software design and systematic engineering validation.

Future Improvements

Computer Vision

Autonomous Navigation

Field Deployment

Conduct longer-duration missions in more challenging marine environments while expanding the platform for future environmental research applications.

Project Summary

SeaSTAR demonstrates the successful development of a complete underwater robotic system capable of remote operation, environmental monitoring, and real-time operator interaction. By combining mechanical design, electrical hardware, embedded systems, and software engineering, the team created an integrated platform capable of collecting environmental data while providing a reliable and intuitive user experience.

The project represents the culmination of our senior capstone experience and showcases the importance of interdisciplinary engineering, iterative testing, and system integration. Through multiple design iterations, pool testing, and harbor deployment, SeaSTAR evolved into a fully operational remotely operated vehicle capable of supporting future environmental monitoring and marine robotics research.
